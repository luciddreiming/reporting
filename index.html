United Airlines - Brief History

Founded

United Airlines was founded in 1926 as Varney Air Lines and officially became United Air Lines in 1931. It is considered one of the oldest commercial airlines in the United States and played a major role in shaping modern aviation.

Key Milestones

In 1930, United hired Ellen Church, the world's first flight attendant. In 2010, United merged with Continental Airlines, forming one of the largest airline companies in the world. The merger expanded international routes and strengthened its global presence.

Innovation

United pioneered the “Hub and Spoke” system which improved passenger connections and route efficiency. It also introduced “Mainliner” service in the 1930s, improving long-distance passenger travel.

Current Status

Headquartered in Chicago, United Airlines operates one of the world's largest route networks. Major hubs include Newark, Chicago, Denver, San Francisco, and Houston.

United Airlines - Routings & Networks

Major Hubs

Chicago (ORD) serves as United’s headquarters and central hub. Denver (DEN) supports western and mountain routes. Guam (GUM) connects Pacific destinations. Houston (IAH) focuses on Latin America. Los Angeles (LAX) handles Pacific operations. Newark (EWR) is a key Atlantic gateway. San Francisco (SFO) connects Asia-Pacific routes. Washington D.C. (IAD) supports government and international travel.

Primary Routes

United Airlines is strongest across the Pacific through San Francisco and across the Atlantic through Newark. In 2026, the airline expanded into niche European destinations such as Split, Croatia and Bari, Italy.

United Airlines - Name Correction Policy

Minor Corrections

Typos with up to three incorrect characters may usually be corrected free of charge if the ticket is entirely operated by United Airlines. Passengers may also cancel and rebook for free within 24 hours of purchase.

Legal Changes

Passengers changing names due to marriage or divorce must provide legal documents such as marriage certificates. Additional fees may apply depending on fare rules.

Contact

Name corrections must be handled directly through United Reservations or the original travel agency where the ticket was purchased.

Air Canada - Brief History

Founded

Air Canada was founded in 1937 as Trans-Canada Air Lines and officially renamed Air Canada in 1965. It became the country’s flag carrier and one of the largest airlines in North America.

Key Milestones

In 1963, Air Canada introduced ReserVec, the world’s first computerized reservation system. In 1990, it became the first airline to offer completely smoke-free flights between North America and Europe. In 2000, it merged with Canadian Airlines, strengthening its position as Canada’s largest airline.

Current Status

Headquartered in Montreal, Air Canada operates an extensive global network. It is known for strong Canada-US routes and popular leisure destinations across the world.

Air Canada - Routings & Networks

Major Hubs

Toronto (YYZ) is Air Canada’s busiest international hub. Montreal (YUL) serves many European destinations. Vancouver (YVR) supports Pacific operations. Calgary (YYC) handles western Canadian routes and domestic connections.

Primary Routes

Air Canada dominates the Canada-US market and continues expanding its leisure routes. In 2026, the airline added destinations such as Tenerife, Roatán, and Mérida to strengthen tourism operations.

Air Canada - Name Correction Policy

3-Character Rule

Air Canada allows corrections for small typographical errors without requiring a complete ticket reissue. Examples include names such as “Sarah” corrected from “Srah”.

Fees

Passengers with Standard or Flex fares may pay correction fees ranging from $60 to $125 CAD depending on fare rules.

Business / Latitude

Business and Latitude fares often include free name corrections. Missing middle names are usually accepted if first and last names match the passenger ID exactly.

24-Hour Rule

Passengers may cancel or correct bookings free of charge within 24 hours after purchase.
